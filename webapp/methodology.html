Metro-Mapping

Drawing Philippine metro areas from open data

City limits often miss the place people actually experience: some Philippine cities contain large rural areas, while other urban regions continue across several local-government boundaries. This project estimates the connected built-up footprint around each city's centre.

1

Divide the region

The city boundary and a 12 km surrounding area are divided into H3 hexagons. Water cells are removed.

2

Identify urban cells

A cell qualifies when it crosses an absolute OpenStreetMap activity threshold or belongs to a WorldPop high-density centre of at least 50,000 people.

3

Connect the metro

The result keeps the urban cells connected to the detected city centre. Short water crossings can be bridged, while ordinary rural gaps remain outside.

How to read the map

The boundary is an exploratory built-up activity footprint. It is useful for comparing the urban extent with administrative limits, but it is not yet a commuting-based official metropolitan boundary. Results that reach the study edge or lack a qualifying population centre are flagged in the app.

Population counts are summed from WorldPop 2020. OpenStreetMap supplies roads, establishments, administrative boundaries and water. Night lights remain a diagnostic layer. Commercial land-price estimates are retained as a separate experiment and are not part of the metro boundary.
